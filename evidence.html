What they
carried out.

The point of teaching is not what I say. It is what students do with it after they leave the room. These are their words, from course reflections and academic seminars at NYU Shanghai.

(Un)Sustainability · Student reflections · 2019
“What I learned during this semester aroused my interest in exploring the environment field and become a better global citizen. This summer, I am going to Dubai for a programme based on sustainability and energy shortage. I really enjoyed this learning journey!”

Stella, EAP: (Un)Sustainability student · Final reflection, May 2019

“Climate change is the core to many world major issues. It’s the base of the iceberg model, the cause and the end for human problems.”

Ziying, EAP: (Un)Sustainability student · Final reflection, May 2019

“I know that the action of individual is enough to make a huge change to the whole situation of environmental protection, and realize that only through the cooperation of three regions can we deal with the global issue.”

Baojia, EAP: (Un)Sustainability student · Final reflection, May 2019

Systems Thinking · Student seminar excerpts · Spring 2025
“The Pomodoro technique is a good way to reorganize our inputs and improve our processes, thus achieving better outputs, encompassing both the bigger picture and the pixels.”

Rebecca, EAP 101 student · Midterm practice seminar, applying systems thinking to time management

“I realized that my previous method of strict time blocks had many problems. By stepping back and analyzing my written time blocks, I could adjust my personal energy levels, environment, and the structure of my work and assignments. I adapted my study routines to align with my natural focus patterns rather than against them.”

Aurelia, EAP 101 student · Connecting the Iceberg Model to personal productivity

“Traditional time management tends to focus on isolated tasks, whereas systems thinking considers multiple factors, such as workload, social relationships, and study habits. The overall benefit could be enhanced efficiency in learning.”

Annie, EAP 101 student · Drawing on academic sources to frame the discussion

“Procrastination, often viewed as a personal failure, can be reframed as a system malfunction requiring structural adjustments rather than merely behavioral motivation.”

Kendrick’s group, EAP 101 · Reframing a common student challenge through a systems lens
