The record

1992 — 1996

German Teacher, Grades 7–12

Springfield High School · Vermont, USA
Small rural public school in southern Vermont. The kind of place where one teacher covers every year group.
Four years teaching secondary German, using a degree minor and genuine fluency to bring a foreign language alive in a community that had very little international exposure. Student-taught in the same district with two very different populations: a program for the county’s most challenging students (behavior graded 80% on not being violent) and Vietnamese refugee children who arrived speaking zero English in rural New England. That dual placement is where the interest in SEN, EAL, and student welfare began.
1997 — 1999

Cambridge English & IELTS Instructor, Kindergarten through Adults

The School for New Languages · Aarau, Switzerland
Private language school in German-speaking Switzerland, serving learners from early childhood to adult professionals.
First international move. Wanted to teach abroad and use the German I’d studied and taught for years.
Taught Cambridge English exam preparation and IELTS across a full age range, from kindergarten to working adults. Built confidence in working across languages, cultures, and developmental stages that would define the next three decades. Living and working in German-speaking Switzerland deepened a bilingual fluency that remains an asset for European roles.
2000 — 2003

Published Columnist · Volunteer Literacy Teacher

The Bangkok Post · Mon Culture & Literature Survival Group · Thailand
The Bangkok Post: Asia’s second-largest English-language newspaper, serving a readership across Southeast Asia.
Wrote a weekly column, Kat’s Window on Thailand, interpreting Thai culture, politics, and daily life for an international readership. This was not freelance blogging: it was a regular published column in a major newspaper, requiring deadline discipline, editorial voice, and the ability to make complex cultural material accessible to a broad audience. Simultaneously volunteered with the Mon Culture and Literature Survival Group, developing literacy materials for refugees in a camp in Sangkhlaburi on the Myanmar border.
2003

IELTS Instructor (part-time)

Taylor College · Sydney, Australia
University pathway college preparing international students for Australian higher education.
IELTS instruction alongside MA TESOL (with distinction) studies at the University of New South Wales.
2004 — 2006

K–12 English Teacher

ILBC (International Language and Business Center) · Mandalay, Myanmar
A K–12 school serving 800 students in Myanmar’s second city. One of only two foreign staff in the entire town.
Wanted to teach where the need was greatest, not where the job was easiest.
Mandalay was the most beautiful and challenging place I have ever lived: limited basic infrastructure, fluctuating politics and policies, and the experience of being one of two foreigners in a city of over a million people. Designed curriculum for every age group across the school. This is where I learned to build something from almost nothing, a skill that would prove useful in every role that followed.
2006 — 2011

Head of EAL · Head of Pastoral, Years 7–13 · Head of PSHE

Bromsgrove International School · Bangkok, Thailand
British international boarding school in Bangkok, CIS accredited. Secondary school with students from across Asia and beyond.
First senior leadership role. The chance to run both the academic (EAL) and pastoral (Years 7–13) systems for an entire secondary school.
This is where leadership began in earnest. Held combined academic and pastoral responsibility across the full secondary school, which meant seeing students as whole people rather than splitting them into “learning” and “welfare” categories. That integration became a defining feature of every subsequent role.
  • Led EAL curriculum design and in-class support across secondary
  • Chaired the PSHCE CIS committee; member of CIS English committee
  • Organized inter-house events, Secondary Book Week, boarding duties
  • Taught IGCSE EAL, IGCSE Literature, mainstream English, IELTS
2011 — 2016

Professional Development Coordinator · Head of EAL · English Teacher

Dulwich College Beijing · China
One of Beijing’s leading international schools. British International School of the Year 2011 (Independent School Awards). IB Diploma averages of 37–38. Part of the Dulwich College International family of schools.
The strongest K–12 leadership chapter. Five years managing a team of sixteen, building systems that scaled, and delivering measurable impact in a genuinely high-performing school.
  • 12% increase in senior school attainment within one year through targeted intervention
  • 300+ admissions interviews per year; built the assessment framework (NGRT, writing, listening, standardized packet)
  • Created the college’s Rolling Professional Development plan
  • Managed and mentored 16 staff (12 TAs, 4 teachers)
  • Founded Language Perfect competitive team: 3rd in the world, 1st place individual (2014)
  • Represented EAL on the Curriculum Committee; aligned EAL vertically and horizontally
  • Originated English for Parents, English for Teaching Assistants, Language Enrichment Group
  • Beta testing for blended IELTS platform (CollegePre / Cambridge University Press / Cambridge Assessment)
2016 — 2018

Director of Student Services, N–13

Harrow International School Shanghai · China
Part of the Harrow family of schools. British curriculum, serving approximately 1,200 students from nursery (18 months) through Year 13.
Left Beijing because of air pollution. Came to Harrow for the opportunity to build whole-school SEN, EAL, and Gifted & Talented support from the ground up.
Built the entire student services department from nothing. This was a ground-up construction project: framework, pathways, systems, training, and culture, all delivered within twelve months for a school spanning ages eighteen months to eighteen years.
  • Built whole-school student services from inception within 12 months (N through Year 13)
  • Trained K–12 faculty in inclusive practice, CLIL methodology, and differentiation
  • Transformed admissions through data-informed assessment; 300+ assessments annually
  • Developed and implemented Individualized Education Plans
  • Coordinated Early Years, Junior, and Senior school transitions and placement
  • Key member of the school leadership team: strategic and operational planning
2018 — 2027

Senior Lecturer · DEI Faculty Chair · Center for Student Belonging Facilitator

NYU Shanghai · China
China’s first Sino-US research university. Third degree-granting campus of the NYU Global Network. Liberal arts curriculum taught entirely in English to approximately 2,000 students from over 70 countries. LEED Gold New Bund Campus.
NYU Shanghai offered the chance to build content-based courses that support EAP students in an EMI transnational education partnership. I choose the theme, I design the course from the ground up, and then I teach it. A dream.
Progressed through Adjunct Faculty, Lecturer, and Senior Lecturer. Designed four interdisciplinary first-year courses, led institutional DEI and belonging work, mentored underrepresented students, and built the curriculum infrastructure for one of the most internationally diverse university programs in China.
  • Designed 4 innovative courses: Future Focused, (Un)Sustainability, Systems Thinking, History of Rock and Roll
  • DEI Faculty Chair: equity strategy, faculty engagement, curriculum review, community-building
  • Led inaugural Culture and Heritage Exposition as Community Connections Chair
  • Workshop facilitator, Center for Student Belonging
  • Co-facilitated LEAD mentorship for first-generation and underrepresented students
  • Recruiter, advisor, and referee for the UN Millennium Fellowship (4,000+ fellows, 290+ campuses, 47 countries)
  • Served on Academic Program Review committees
  • Co-founded NYU Shanghai Sustainability Committee (LEED Gold campus)
The most important aspect of my work is whom I teach, not what I teach.
